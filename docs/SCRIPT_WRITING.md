# Script Writing Bible — Story (Kahini) First

> The #1 rule of this studio: **a video is a STORY, not a slideshow of cute scenes.**
> If there is no kahini — no want, no obstacle, no climax, no change — there is no video.

## 1. Build the Kahini Before Any Dialogue

Answer these 7 questions first (Master SKILL §5.5). Write them at the top of every script:

1. What does the hero **want**? (one specific, visible thing)
2. **Why** does it matter? (the heart — friendship, a promise, someone's feelings)
3. **Obstacle 1** — first try fails (funny)
4. **Obstacle 2** — it gets worse (feelings rise: time, weather, misunderstanding)
5. **Low moment** — hero almost gives up (quiet, 2-3s in Shorts)
6. **Climax idea** — clever solution using something shown earlier
7. **Payoff + change** — who is different at the end?

Formula: `{Hero} wants {want} because {heart}, but {obstacle 1} and {obstacle 2} stop them, until {climax idea} — and in the end {change}.`

### Weak vs. Strong (learn the difference)
- ❌ WEAK (scenes, no story): "Friends fly a kite. It gets stuck. A frog frees it. Picnic."
  Nothing is wanted, nothing is felt, the solution arrives from outside, nobody changes.
- ✅ STRONG (kahini): "Momo made a kite for shy little Pipo, who has never flown one.
  The wind steals it (Obstacle 1). It lands in the high tree and rain clouds come —
  the paper kite will melt! (Obstacle 2, stakes + clock). Pipo whispers, 'I can't fly
  that high…' (Low moment). Then Pipo remembers: he CAN fly — he just never believed it.
  He flutters up, wobbly, brave (Climax — the solution was inside the hero).
  Kite saved before the rain. Pipo lands, shining: 'I did it. I really flew!' (Change: courage)."
  Same kite. Completely different video. **Always write the second kind.**

## 2. Suspense for Kids (Gentle, Always Resolved)
Keep ONE question open until the climax:
- Mystery object: "What is glowing inside the paper cave?"
- Race against gentle clock: "Can they get home before the paper stars sleep?"
- Misunderstanding: "Why did Momo hide when she saw me?" (answer: a surprise!)
- Promise: "Will Pipo keep his promise to the little seed?"
Every scene must feed the question: a clue, a setback, or a near-answer.

## 3. Structure Templates

### Short 60s (6 clips) — Kahini in miniature
1. **Want + Hook (0-10s):** hero, want and heart in one line + movement.
2. **Obstacle 1 (10-20s):** first try fails funny.
3. **Obstacle 2 (20-30s):** worse — stakes/clock/misunderstanding appears.
4. **Low Moment + Idea (30-40s):** quiet beat → hero remembers/discovers the climax idea.
5. **Climax (40-50s):** hero does the brave/clever thing. Biggest visual moment.
6. **Payoff + Change + Lesson (50-60s):** want achieved, change spoken, goodbye/loop.

### Long 5 min (10 scenes) — same spine, more room
Want/Hook → World & Friends → Obstacle 1 → Small Win (hope) → Obstacle 2 (bigger) →
Low Moment (a full gentle scene + soft song) → Idea (from an earlier detail) →
Climax (everyone's small talents combine) → Payoff → Change + Lesson + Next-episode tease.
Add ONE subplot only in Long: a friend wants a small thing too, resolved inside the climax.

## 4. Dialogue That Sounds Like Talking, Not Reading
- Short lines (Master §6.3 word limits). Real kids interrupt, repeat, exclaim: "Wait— look!", "Me? I can?"
- Give each character a **verbal fingerprint**: a catchphrase + a habit
  (Pipo repeats the last word when scared: "High… too high…"; Momo hums when she thinks).
- Emotion words are spoken, not narrated: not "Pipo was sad" but Pipo, quietly: "Nobody… came."
- Questions drive scenes. Answers change things. No line may repeat what the picture already shows.
- Read every line out loud while writing. If you run out of breath, the line is too long.

## 5. Emotion Beats (plan them like jokes — one per clip minimum)
Wonder → Giggle → Worry → Hope → Giggle → Brave → Proud → Warm.
A flat emotion line = a boring video, however pretty the paper looks.

## 6. The Rewrite Pass (mandatory, Master §6.4)
After the draft, ask of every clip: (a) Does the kahini move here? (b) Is a feeling
happening? (c) Is the best line of the clip quotable by a 5-year-old tomorrow?
Rewrite any clip that fails two of the three. Then score /40 and only deliver at 32+,
with Kahini completeness at 5/5.

## 7. Common Failures — Reject and Rewrite
- Scenes connected by "and then… and then…" instead of "but… so…"
- Solution arrives from a new character/object never shown before
- Lesson announced by a narrator while characters learned nothing
- Everyone happy all the time (no worry = no payoff)
- Ending that repeats the middle instead of mirroring the opening with a change
